Edibles After the Beverage Boom: Gummies, Ratios, Fast-Acting, and the Pack Ladder

Published November 11, 2026 · CannActuality Research Desk · For licensed vendors and product teams

Key takeaways

  • California edibles fell 3.8% year over year in April 2026 (Headset data). The latest month reads differently: October 2026 edible sales rose 10.0% year over year, just behind the market's 11.0% (Headset). Nationally, edibles were flat at −0.2% for the 12 months ending September 2026, holding 11.8% of US sales (Headset).
  • Gummies are the category. They hold 88.6% of Colorado edible dollars (Headset, January–July 2026), and every one of California's ten best-selling edibles in Headset's October 3, 2026 snapshot was a gummy. Chocolates held 6% of edible sales in BDSA-tracked markets in Q1 2025, the same share as beverages.
  • The growth is in ratios, not in THC-only products. California minor-cannabinoid edibles grew 8.2% to $115.2 million in January–August 2026 while THC-only edibles were flat. In Colorado, THC-only edibles fell 14.1% while multi-cannabinoid products grew 0.7% (Headset).
  • Fast-acting is a small but concentrated premium rung. It was 3% of a $2.57 billion US gummy market in 2024 (BDSA data, in a report co-presented with a fast-acting technology vendor), yet fast-acting SKUs make up 29.4% of one gummy brand's October 2026 sales (Headset).
  • Most of the market buys the same core pack: 100 mg in ten pieces. Development money earns its return by building clear rungs around that pack, through more pieces per pack, a ratio or need-state formula, or a fast-acting format, rather than by adding more THC-only flavors.

The headline number, in context

The figure that started this brief checks out. Headset data reported by KOLAS (May 21, 2026) shows California edible sales of $38.06 million in April 2026, down 3.8% year over year. The whole market fell 7.1% that month, so edibles held up better than flower (−10.6%) and concentrates (−9.6%). The Marijuana Herald (May 5, 2026), also using Headset data, confirms the $38 million April figure.

That single month is now out of date, and the more recent numbers are better. Headset's California market page (data snapshot November 9, 2026) puts October 2026 edible sales at $37.24 million, up 10.0% year over year. The total market rose 11.0% and beverages rose 22.3%. Over a longer window, Headset's California presentation at IgniteIt (October 7, 2026) showed edible units up 5.5% and dollars up 2.9% for January–August 2026. Units grew faster than dollars, so each unit is bringing in less revenue.

For product teams, the takeaway is that edibles are a large, mature category that is roughly holding its size, not one in collapse. The beverage boom did not take away edibles' growth. Edible growth simply moved into particular formulas and pack types, which the rest of this brief maps.

Gummies are the category; chocolate is a brand play

Every recent dataset points the same way:

  • Colorado: gummies are 88.6% of edible category dollars (Headset Colorado deep dive, August 20, 2026, January–July 2026 data).
  • BDSA-tracked markets, Q1 2025: candy, the subcategory that includes gummies, held 79% of edible sales. Chocolates held 6%, beverages 6% and pills 5% (BDSA, April 28, 2025). BDSA counts beverages inside edibles. Even so, after years of beverage headlines, drinks were the same size as chocolate.
  • California's best sellers: all ten top edibles in Headset's 30-day snapshot dated October 3, 2026 were gummies sold in 10- or 20-piece packs.
  • Concentration: one brand, Wyld, accounts for roughly 15% of all US edible sales, according to Headset's edible category page (October 2025–September 2026).

Chocolate still works when the brand is built on it. About 75.4% of Kiva Chocolate's October 2026 sales came from chocolate bars in its top ten products, mostly 20-piece bars. The brand also offers higher-milligram bars where state rules allow larger packages (Headset, snapshot November 3, 2026). Taken together, the data says a new chocolate or baked line needs a brand reason to exist. A new gummy line needs a reason to be chosen over an established gummy leader.

The shopper base is broader than in most categories. Women account for 42% of edible dollars, compared with 32% of flower, and Baby Boomers and Gen X together make up more than half of edible spend (Headset). In California, Gen Z edible sales grew 18.4% in January–August 2026 (Headset, IgniteIt).

Where the growth is: ratio and minor-cannabinoid gummies

The clearest finding in 2026 data is that THC-only edibles are flat or shrinking, while multi-cannabinoid edibles are holding or growing.

  • California: minor-cannabinoid edibles (CBN, CBG, THCV, CBC) grew 8.2% to $115.2 million in January–August 2026, while THC-only edibles were flat (Headset, IgniteIt, October 7, 2026).
  • Colorado: edibles fell 8.1% year over year, and Headset attributes the decline almost entirely to THC-only products, which fell 14.1%. Multi-cannabinoid products grew 0.7% and now hold 44.3% of category sales. Their share is 52.2% in Washington. Of the added cannabinoids, CBN and CBG are holding or gaining, while CBD fell 10.6% (Headset, August 20, 2026).
  • California best sellers: six of the top ten edibles in Headset's October 3, 2026 snapshot contain CBN alongside THC, often with CBD as well. The top two are both ratio gummies.
  • Brand level: ratio SKUs make up about 40% of Wyld's October 2026 sales. The five ratio items in its top ten are 1:1:1 CBD/CBN/THC, 2:1 THC/CBN, 1:1 CBG/THC, 1:1 CBD/THC and 20:1 CBD/THC (Headset, snapshot November 10, 2026). At Jams, 2:1:1 and 1:1:1 formulas make up 31.8% of sales (Headset, snapshot November 3, 2026).

Several leading ratio SKUs carry need-state names, so the formula and the label are developed together. When making any label or marketing claim, product teams should treat need-state naming as a regulated claims question.

Fast-acting: small share, strong positioning

"Fast-acting" in this brief is a marketing and formulation label, tied to proprietary infusion processes such as Azuca's TiME INFUSION. It is not a claim about how a product performs. The best public sales read comes from a 2025 report co-presented by BDSA and Azuca. Azuca sells fast-acting infusion technology, so treat the framing with that interest in mind. As reported by The Marijuana Herald (May 27, 2025) and in Azuca's release (May 28, 2025), the report's figures are:

  • Fast-acting gummies were about 3% of the $2.57 billion US gummy market in 2024, based on BDSA retail data across 14 markets.
  • Fast-acting gummies sold at higher original and sell-through prices than conventional gummies.
  • Some brands with fast-acting lines drew as much as 50% of their sales from those products.

Recent brand data supports the "small but concentrated" reading. Four fast-acting 20-piece SKUs make up 29.4% of Jams' October 2026 sales. Three items in Wana's "Quick" line make up 11.2% of that brand's sales (Headset, snapshots November 3 and November 10, 2026). Fast-acting works best as a defined premium rung inside an established gummy line, not as a standalone brand promise.

Low potency and pack-count architecture

Pack design is limited by regulation first. In California, an edible cannabis product may contain no more than 10 mg THC per serving and 100 mg THC per package (Cal. Code Regs. tit. 4, § 17304). Headset describes the standard US edible purchase as a 100 mg pack of ten 10 mg gummies. With the THC per package capped, the main variables left are piece count and the other cannabinoids in the formula.

  • Piece count is a lever: the leading fast-acting SKUs at Jams and Wana fit 100 mg into 20 pieces instead of ten. Kiva's top bars are 20-piece formats.
  • Low-THC ratio products sell: Wyld's 20:1 CBD/THC 20-pack contains 10 mg of THC in total and is 4.5% of brand sales. Wana's top-ten list includes a 5:1:1:1 formula with 20 mg of THC per pack (Headset).
  • Hemp channel limits: in California, AB 8 has generally treated intoxicating hemp products as cannabis since January 1, 2026. Food use of CBD or CBN isolate (above 99% purity, no THC) remains possible under CDPH authorization (CDPH AB 8 FAQ). Federally, fully synthetic cannabinoids lose hemp status on November 12, 2026. The 0.4 mg total-THC-per-container limit takes effect December 11, 2026 (Foley Hoag, September 15, 2026). Low-potency hemp gummies sold outside dispensaries need a licensed-channel plan.

The price ladder, described structurally

This brief reports no price points. The structure of the ladder can be seen in pack design and in Headset's peer benchmarking, which compares each product against others of the same product type and package size. That method means a tier is set by format and pack, not by flavor.

TierTypical architecture in current best sellersEvidence of where it sits
ValueTHC-only, single-cannabinoid 100 mg packsTHC-only edibles are flat in California and down 14.1% in Colorado. California's discount rate rose from 20.3% to 23.1% of shelf value in January–August 2026 (Headset).
Core100 mg 10-pack from a category-leading gummy brandHeadset describes this pack as the standard edible purchase. Several THC-only Wyld 10-packs rank in California's top ten.
PremiumRatio or minor-cannabinoid 10-packs, fast-acting 20-packs, branded chocolate barsRatio items are about 40% of Wyld's sales. Headset's peer comparison places Wana's top ten above the same-type, same-pack peer average. Fast-acting sells at a premium to conventional gummies (BDSA/Azuca).

Discounting is the main risk to this ladder. When the discount rate rises, premium and core products are pushed toward value. Headset's 2026 advice to California retailers is to protect margin and increase basket attachment rather than deepen discounts.

What this means for product teams

  • Put new edible R&D money into ratio formulas, not more THC-only flavors. In both California and Colorado, multi-cannabinoid edibles held or grew while THC-only edibles stalled. CBN and CBG are the additions holding up, and CBD-only additions are fading.
  • Develop gummies first. At 79% to 88.6% of edible dollars, the gummy is the default. Develop chocolate or baked goods only when your brand has a reason to own that format.
  • Treat fast-acting as a defined premium rung. It holds a small share of the market but a large share of some brands' sales. Use "fast-acting" only as a marketing label, and keep any onset claims within what state rules allow.
  • Build the ladder from pack architecture. Within a 100 mg cap, piece count (10 vs 20), formula (THC-only vs ratio) and format (standard vs fast-acting) are the rungs. Each SKU should have one clear reason to sit on its rung.
  • Budget for a flat category with shifting mix. Plan for a US category that is about level overall. Base the launch case on taking share from THC-only basics, and update it with each month of state data. The April figures were already out of date by October.
  • Close out hemp-channel exposure before December 11, 2026. Any gummy sold outside licensed dispensaries needs a documented licensed-channel path or an exit plan.
