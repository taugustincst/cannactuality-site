Retail Buyer Brief: Assortment Planning for a Flat Market

Published November 23, 2026 · CannActuality Research Desk · For licensed vendors and product teams

Key takeaways

  • The market a buyer is planning into is flat in dollars and growing in units. Across the 16 state markets Headset tracks, sales totaled $24.6 billion in the 12 months ending September 2026, up 1.5 percent year over year, while unit sales grew 4.8 percent and the average basket fell 4.2 percent to $47.03 (Headset, updated October 3, 2026).
  • California, the largest market, is the flat-market case study: sales were essentially flat at −0.1 percent in January–August 2026 after 50 straight months of year-over-year decline, gross sales rose 3.5 percent, and a discount rate that climbed from 20.3 percent to 23.1 percent pulled net sales back to flat (Headset, September 30, 2026).
  • Category roles are diverging. Nationally, flower holds 39.1 percent of sales but fell 0.7 percent; pre-rolls grew 10.7 percent and beverages grew 9.3 percent, while capsules fell 22.3 percent, topicals 13.1 percent, and tinctures 7.0 percent (Headset, October 3, 2026).
  • The price ladder is being rebuilt by pack size, not by sticker cuts alone. Packaged flower averaged $3.56 per gram in September 2026, but a single gram averaged $8.09 per gram against $1.71 per gram for an ounce, and the average discount rate rose from 23.6 percent to 27.1 percent (Headset, October 3, 2026).
  • The shelf is consolidating whether buyers plan for it or not: 207 California brands — 17 percent — stopped selling in a year when total dollars did not move, and beverage brand count fell from 71 to 46 while revenue per surviving brand rose 80.6 percent (Headset, September 30 and October 7, 2026).

The planning problem: flat dollars, rising units, rising discounts

Assortment planning used to start from growth: add doors, add brands, add SKUs, and let a rising market absorb the mistakes. The 2026 data removes that cushion. Nationally, the growth that exists is geographic, not general — Ohio grew 26.7 percent and New York 22.1 percent in the 12 months ending September 2026, together adding roughly $610 million, while Nevada fell 12.0 percent and every West Coast market declined in dollar terms (Headset). Exactly half of the 16 tracked markets grew. A buyer in a mature market is not planning into the national average; they are planning into their own state's line in that table.

The forward view says this is the planning horizon, not a one-year dip. BDSA's April 2026 forecast, as analyzed by Tom Adams Research, puts the 2025–2030 worldwide compound annual growth rate at 4.3 percent and U.S. growth at just 3.6 percent compounded through 2030, with ten mature U.S. adult-use markets — including Colorado, Nevada, Michigan, Illinois, and Massachusetts — forecast to contract between 2025 and 2030 and shed a combined $781 million (BDSA Final Forecast, April 2026, via Adams Research, April 20, 2026). The mechanism BDSA names is price erosion: across the twelve U.S. markets where BDSA has ongoing retail sales tracking, average retail prices fell 15 percent from 2023 to 2025.

California shows what "flat" actually feels like at store level. The state did $2.7 billion in January–August 2026 and ended 50 consecutive months of decline with four straight months of growth — May +1.2 percent, June +0.7 percent, July +0.3 percent, August +2.7 percent (Headset, October 7, 2026). But units sit 14 percent above the 2021 average while dollars sit 23 percent below it, and the recovery in gross sales was handed back at the register as discounting climbed. In April 2026, in the middle of that turn, California tracked sales were about $311 million across more than 17.3 million units, down 7.1 percent year over year, with the average item price down to $17.91 from $18.89 (Headset data reported by KOLAS, 2026). The buyer's job in this market is not to find growth in the headline. It is to decide, category by category, which roles still earn their space.

Basket data confirms the roles. Flower appears in 37.3 percent of all dispensary baskets, pre-rolls in 32.7 percent, vapor pens in 30.0 percent, and edibles in 16.9 percent (Headset, 12 months ending September 2026). The two core categories and the growth engine are also the three most common basket items — cutting depth there to fund experiments elsewhere taxes the trips the store actually gets. The reverse is true at the tail: capsules, topicals, and tinctures together are under 1.3 percent of national sales and all three are shrinking at high single digits or worse. In a flat market, tail space is the budget for everything else.

California's internal mix shows where a mature shelf drifts if the buyer lets it. Flower takes 31.1 percent of California dollars against 38.5 percent across U.S. Insights markets, vapor pens take 29.3 percent against 25.6 percent, and beverages run at 2.5 percent against 1.3 percent nationally (Headset, January–August 2026). Inside vapor pens, all-in-one disposables took $350.7 million in the first eight months of 2026, up $41.7 million, while cartridges took $425.6 million, down $35.9 million; disposable share of category dollars moved from 34.5 percent in 2024 to 40.1 percent in 2025 to 45.1 percent in 2026. The five segments that added the most California dollars were disposable vapes (+$41.7 million), specialty and infused flower (+$11.0 million), hybrid single-strain pre-rolls (+$9.1 million), gummies (+$8.8 million), and indica flower (+$8.3 million), with carbonated beverages adding $5.9 million on a $12.7 million base. Assortment follows those segment moves, or it defends a mix the shopper has already left.

The price ladder: pack architecture is doing the discounting

The most important pricing fact for a 2026 buyer is that shoppers are trading into larger packs, and the per-gram ladder rewards them steeply for it. In September 2026, a single-gram flower package averaged $8.09 ($8.09 per gram), an eighth averaged $20.23 ($5.78 per gram), a quarter averaged $28.82 ($4.12 per gram), a half ounce averaged $41.72 ($2.98 per gram), and an ounce averaged $47.83 ($1.71 per gram) — the ounce costs about one fifth of the single-gram rate per gram (Headset, October 3, 2026). Average item prices by category in the same month ran from flower at $23.92 and vapor pens at $22.02 down to beverages at $8.17 and pre-rolls at $8.08, with edibles at $10.50 and concentrates at $17.73.

California's flower data shows the shift in motion. Flower fell from $4.62 per gram in 2024 to $3.79 in 2026, a decline of 18 percent, but the eighth's share of flower dollars fell from 55.5 percent in 2024 to 46.1 percent in 2026 while the 7-gram tier rose from 8.3 percent to 14.3 percent and the 14-gram tier added another four points (Headset, September 30, 2026). A California eighth sells for $6.34 per gram against $3.98 for a quarter, $2.75 for a half ounce, and $1.81 for an ounce. The category is not simply cheaper; the mix is moving up the pack ladder, and a flower set still built around the eighth is stocked for a shopper who is leaving.

Discounting is the second ladder, and it is moving the wrong way. Nationally, the average discount rate rose from 23.6 percent to 27.1 percent of shelf value in September 2026 (Headset). In California, gross sales rose 3.5 percent in January–August 2026 while net sales were flat because the discount rate climbed from 20.3 percent to 23.1 percent — and California still discounts less than Arizona (41.1 percent), Washington (37.5 percent), Illinois (33.7 percent), and Nevada (30.8 percent), while discounting more than Oregon (16.2 percent), Colorado (15.9 percent), and Massachusetts (13.2 percent) (Headset, October 7, 2026). For the buyer, the implication is direct: open-to-buy spent defending a full-price ladder that the register then discounts by a quarter is not a ladder. Build the rungs into pack size and format — single and eighth for trial and convenience, quarter and larger for the value rung, infused and specialty for the premium rung — and reserve promotion for clearing mistakes, not for making the core assortment work.

One more constraint belongs in every ladder review: the same rung is not the same price in every state. The average eighth ranged from $12.74 in Oregon to $29.54 in Ohio in September 2026, a 2.3-times spread (Headset). Multi-state buyers should set ladder architecture centrally — the sequence of rungs and the gaps between them — and let each market price the rungs locally.

Pre-roll allocation: fund the growth engine, in the formats that are winning

Pre-rolls are the clearest allocation decision in the brief. Nationally the category grew 10.7 percent to $3.96 billion and 16.1 percent of sales in the 12 months ending September 2026 (Headset). The longer view agrees: Custom Cones USA's State of the Pre-Roll Market 2026, built on Headset 2025 data, put the category at $3.6 billion across more than 383 million units, with revenue up 9.8 percent and units up 18.6 percent against 1.5 percent growth for the broader market (Custom Cones USA, April 15, 2026).

Inside the category, two format facts should drive the buy:

  • Multi-packs carry the top of the chart. Multi-packs represent 48.5 percent of pre-roll SKUs nationally, and 90 of the top 100 products are sold in bundled formats; the leading configuration, a 2.5-gram five-pack, generated more than $600 million, while the 1-gram single remained the top individual product format at $1.44 billion (Custom Cones USA, April 15, 2026). In California, multi-packs took 64.9 percent of category dollars with units up 10.2 percent, nearly double the 5.5 percent unit growth on singles (Headset, January–August 2026).
  • The infused premium is market-specific, not automatic. Nationally, infused pre-rolls generated $1.68 billion, 47 percent of category revenue, and the fastest growth in the category (Custom Cones USA). In California, infused holds 57.9 percent of category dollars but grew only 0.5 percent in January–August 2026 while non-infused grew 10.6 percent, and the fastest-growing California segments were single-strain lines — indica +20 percent, sativa +9.2 percent, hybrid +8.4 percent (Headset). A mature-market buyer who copies the national infused playbook overbuys the premium rung and underbuys the accessible multi-pack the local shopper is actually moving to.

California pre-rolls overall grew 4.5 percent in dollars and 7.3 percent in units in January–August 2026, and basket penetration rose from 30.7 percent to 31.7 percent (Headset). The allocation read: expand pre-roll space, weight it to multi-packs, keep the 1-gram single as the trial rung, and set the infused share from your own market's segment growth — not from the national headline.

Beverage allocation: a small, deliberate bet with a closing window

Beverages are 1.2 percent of national sales — $299 million in the 12 months ending September 2026 — and grew 9.3 percent (Headset). California, the mature-market leading indicator, runs the category at 2.5 percent of dollars, close to twice the national rate: beverage sales grew 17 percent to $64.9 million in January–August 2026, units grew 20.3 percent, and basket penetration reached 5.6 percent, the highest in the country, with carbonated beverages up 47 percent and tea and coffee nearly tripling (Headset, September 30 and October 7, 2026). In April 2026, beverages were the only major California category growing, up 8.0 percent in sales and 14.6 percent in units (Headset data reported by KOLAS).

Two facts argue for funding the bet now rather than later. First, retention: beverage brands keep 60.7 percent of their customers on average, against 34.3 percent across brands and categories and 30.9 percent for pre-roll brands (Headset, October 2026). A beverage slot that works repeats in a way most of the shelf does not. Second, concentration: California beverage brands fell from 71 to 46 in a year, the top three brands now hold 71.4 percent of the category, and revenue per surviving brand rose 80.6 percent (Headset). The category is choosing its winners while it grows. The buyer implication is a focused bet — a tight set of proven brands in the formats showing growth, given real placement — rather than a broad, shallow beverage wall assembled after the concentration has finished.

Who is pulling the category matters for placement and ranging. Gen Z is the only generation adding dollars in California, and its beverage spend grew 22.9 percent in January–August 2026, alongside edibles +18.4 percent and pre-rolls +9.6 percent (Headset, October 7, 2026). Nationally, Gen Z's share of spending has climbed from 18.4 percent to 21.7 percent in two years, while Millennials account for 42.7 percent of U.S. spending (Headset, 12 months ending September 2026). Beverage and pre-roll expansion is, in part, assortment following the cohort that is still growing.

Cutting the tail: the math that funds the plan

Every expansion above has to be funded from somewhere, and in a flat market it is funded from the tail. The concentration evidence is blunt: across Treez client stores, about 12 percent of products make up 50 percent of sales, stores carrying under 350 SKUs consistently record more items per ticket than any other group, and two thirds of the small number of retailers achieving over $100 per ticket work with limited assortments under 500 SKUs — against an average retailer assortment of around 1,000 SKUs on the platform (Treez, updated September 11, 2026). California's brand exit wave — 1,221 brands in January–August 2025 down to 1,014 in the same period of 2026 — means part of the tail is disappearing on its own; the share it frees is landing on the challenger tier below the leader, where Jetty Extracts, CAM, Raw Garden, and Kingpen are growing 15 percent to 56 percent while the leader, STIIIZY at 8.4 percent share, grows flat (Headset, September 30, 2026).

Basket economics set the priority order for what the freed space and dollars should buy. California averages 2.2 items and $40.67 per trip, among the lowest in the country, against Colorado at 3.0 items and Michigan at 5.3 (Headset, January–August 2026). Nationally the average basket is $47.03 across 2.74 items (Headset, 12 months ending September 2026). For a California buyer, attachment — a second and third item that fits the trip already happening — is worth more than another point of discount on the first item. That is the commercial case for putting the tail's budget into pre-roll multi-packs and a focused beverage set: both are proven attach and repeat formats in the categories still growing.

What this means for product teams

  • Assign every category a role before you assign it a budget. On the national evidence: flower and vapor pens are the cores to protect, pre-rolls are the growth engine to expand, edibles are a hold-and-focus category, concentrates are an enthusiast niche, beverages are the expansion bet, and capsules, topicals, and tinctures — all shrinking, together under 1.3 percent of sales — are where the space comes from (Headset, October 3, 2026).
  • Rebuild the flower ladder around larger packs. The eighth's share of California flower dollars fell from 55.5 percent to 46.1 percent in two years while 7-gram and 14-gram formats gained, and the national per-gram ladder runs from $8.09 for a single gram to $1.71 for an ounce (Headset). Stock the ladder the shopper is climbing, not the one from 2024.
  • Stop financing the assortment with discounts. California's gross sales rose 3.5 percent and net sales were flat because the discount rate climbed to 23.1 percent; nationally it reached 27.1 percent (Headset). Set a discount ceiling per category in the buying plan, and treat breaches as an assortment failure to fix — wrong rung, wrong pack, wrong brand — not as a promotion to extend.
  • Weight pre-roll open-to-buy to multi-packs, and set infused share locally. Multi-packs are 90 of the national top 100 pre-roll products and 64.9 percent of California category dollars; but California non-infused pre-rolls grew 10.6 percent against 0.5 percent for infused in January–August 2026, the reverse of the national picture (Custom Cones USA; Headset). Buy the format trend nationally and the segment trend locally.
  • Make the beverage bet small, focused, and now. Beverages grew 17 percent in California to a 2.5 percent share, beverage brands retain 60.7 percent of customers, and the California brand count has already fallen from 71 to 46 (Headset). A tight set of proven brands with real placement beats a broad wall built after the category has concentrated.
  • Cut the tail on a schedule, and spend the proceeds on attachment. With about 12 percent of products producing 50 percent of sales in Treez store data and California baskets at 2.2 items, delist in planned waves, protect the proven sellers in every niche you keep, and measure success in items per ticket and repeat rate — not in SKU count.
  • For brands pitching buyers: arrive as a role, not a SKU. In a consolidating market — 207 California brands exited in a flat year — the pitch that lands names the rung, the pack architecture, and the category role the product fills, and shows segment growth in the buyer's own market. Challengers growing 15 percent to 56 percent in California are winning shelf that a flat leader is not defending (Headset, September 30, 2026).
